Algebraic Varieties

An algebraic variety is the set of points where one or more polynomial equations vanish.

V = {x | f₁(x) = ··· = fₖ(x) = 0}

This gallery shows the real points of hypersurfaces f(x,y,z)=0. Algebraic geometers often study the richer complex and projective varieties; an affine view may therefore clip sheets that continue through infinity.

Where ∇f is nonzero it is perpendicular to the surface and supplies the shading normal. Points with both f=0 and ∇f=0 are singular, optionally highlighted here in red.

Substituting a camera ray o+td into f produces a one-variable polynomial in t. The GPU samples and refines its nearest positive real root—without pretending that f is a distance field.

Each camera ray first intersects a conservative bounding sphere, producing a finite interval in which the surface can occur. WGSL then evaluates the polynomial along o+td at fixed samples and keeps the nearest sign change.

The bracket is refined with guarded secant steps and bisection. To recover tangent and even-multiplicity roots that never change sign, local minima of |f| / max(|∇f|, ε) seed clamped Newton steps using the ray derivative ∇f · d.

Analytic gradients provide normals and reveal candidate singularities where |∇f| is small. Linear HDR lighting and specular highlights are compressed by an ACES-like display transform.

A reusable GPUCommandGraph render node encodes the fullscreen implicit-surface pass. The polynomial catalog supplies only field and gradient functions; intersection and shading remain geometry-agnostic.

This is numerical root finding, not a proof: fixed sampling can miss tightly clustered roots, and singular or grazing intersections are ill-conditioned. The polynomial value is never treated as a signed-distance field.

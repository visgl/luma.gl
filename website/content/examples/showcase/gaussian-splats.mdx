The default scene places two copies of a captured cat figurine at different depths. On WebGPU,
the transparent pane moves between them. The depth-interval shader plugin draws the splats behind
the pane first, blends the glass, then draws the splats in front. Use **Moving tinted glass** in
**Settings** to compare the result, and drag to orbit the scene.

The example downloads one 2 MB [cat figurine SPZ scan](https://github.com/raymondyfei/SharedTestModels/blob/92aec9410cd3cbc661d4c4e3c5e8c78157357988/a_cute_cat_float32.spz)
from SharedTestModels under [CC0 1.0 Universal](https://github.com/raymondyfei/SharedTestModels/blob/92aec9410cd3cbc661d4c4e3c5e8c78157357988/LICENSE).

Turn off the pane to inspect standard WebGPU rendering in **GPU Graph** and **GPU Trace**. Choose
**Synthetic chromatic showcase** to stream the generated scene, or **CPU depth ordering** to
compare the portable renderer. WebGL2 uses attribute-backed rendering. The example keeps
format parsing in loaders.gl and uses [`@luma.gl/engine/arrow`](/docs/api-reference/arrow) to
convert decoded Gaussian batches into GPU data.

Spherical-harmonic DC colors remain unclamped floating-point radiance from loading through GPU
rendering. Standard displays use configurable exposure and tone mapping, while compatible WebGPU
displays can present directly to an extended-range `rgba16float` canvas.

To explore complete captured scenes, open the
[Gaussian Splat Viewer](/examples/showcase/gaussian-splat-viewer). It starts with the
741,883-splat Train capture and offers Truck, Dr Johnson, Playroom, GitHub fallback, and custom
public source URLs without requiring a local loaders.gl checkout.

The standalone example also supports an optional neighboring loaders.gl 5 alpha checkout. Start it
with `VITE_LOADERS_GL_ROOT=/path/to/loaders.gl` and open `?loaders=local`; use
`?loaders=local&scene=fixture` for the small parser test asset.

Known GraphDECO captures use scene-specific camera-up conventions instead of assuming every scene
is Z-up; Truck also starts from its published camera position. Foreground-based framing keeps the
subject visible, manual orbit and zoom persist while additional batches stream in, and stationary
scenes avoid unnecessary redraws.

The Satellite Raster Lab generates a deterministic, entirely synthetic
earth-observation scene with red and near-infrared reflectance bands, farmland,
vegetation, waterways, cloud cover, and out-of-footprint nodata. Switch between
the derived vegetation index and its source bands, apply Gaussian or box
smoothing, adjust the kernel radius or Gaussian sigma, change analytical
contrast or gamma, select pixels above a live classification threshold, or
increase the denominator epsilon to reject unstable normalized-difference
measurements. An optional Otsu control derives its threshold directly from a
pre-threshold GPU histogram before classifying the same resident pixels.
Every control changes the GPU-computed distribution, selected-pixel count,
observed extent, and mean when its underlying values or selection change.

The demonstration uses the optional `@luma.gl/experimental/luraster` entry point.
`GPURasterNDVI` computes `(nearInfrared - red) / (nearInfrared + red)` directly
from GPU-resident source bands while intersecting source validity and exact raw
nodata. `GPURasterHistogram` composes a masked reduction with a GPU-domain
histogram. `GPURasterGaussianBlur` and `GPURasterBoxBlur` smooth the active
spectral band through separate horizontal and vertical GPU passes, reflecting
raster borders and renormalizing valid neighboring samples without filling
masked center pixels. `GPURasterContrast` then transforms that resident band
on-device;
`GPURasterThreshold` writes a genuine per-pixel selection mask; and
`GPURasterStatistics` calculates masked count, sum, mean, minimum, and maximum.
`GPURasterOtsuThreshold` can select the threshold from a separate baseline
histogram without introducing a CPU synchronization point.
The histogram consumes that same mask and GPU-resident extent, so excluded
pixels disappear from both the map selection and distribution. Every dependent
compute pass is encoded into one explicitly submitted `GPUCommandGraph`.

The map renderer consumes the original reflectance bands, derived vegetation
index, contrast-adjusted active band, and validity flags directly from their GPU
buffers. Only the two-value extent, 48 histogram bins, valid count, sum, and
mean, and optional automatic threshold—216 bytes total—are copied back after
submission to update visible summary
statistics. Layer switching, smoothing mode, kernel radius, Gaussian sigma,
contrast, gamma, thresholding, and epsilon rebuild their specialized analysis
graph while keeping every source and output raster buffer resident; source
pixels are never downloaded.

Choose Gaussian smoothing to reduce local sensor noise while weighting nearby
observations most strongly; choose box smoothing when a uniformly weighted
local mean is the intended measurement. Unlike a display-only image effect,
these mask-aware compute stages change the values consumed by histograms,
statistics, and Otsu classification. Their separable kernels evaluate `O(r)`
taps per pixel for radius `r`, compared with `O(r²)` for direct square
convolution; actual elapsed time depends on the workload and GPU.

All imagery, coordinates, and terrain are synthetic. The visualization is a
false-color illustration, not a real satellite acquisition, true-color image,
georeferenced location, or scientifically calibrated product. Derivative
filters, morphology, segmentation, contour extraction, and tiled imagery shown
in the interface remain planned capabilities.

Three synthetic telemetry channels contain **1,048,576 samples each**, sampled at 10 Hz over
29 hours. Request latency, queue depth and error rate share a brief incident and a telemetry gap.

- Choose **1. Inspect incident** to reveal a correlated burst across all three charts.
- Choose **2. Hide peaks**: isolated spikes disappear when a bin's first observation misses
  them. The exact envelope preserves every finite minimum and maximum.
- Choose **3. Inspect missing data**: empty bins stay empty, while amber bins contain both observations and
  missing samples. An envelope summarizes a bin; it does not reconstruct continuity within it.
- Drag a time interval or scroll to zoom. Use the Zoom slider, then Pan to move through history; reset with **Full history**.
- Click a chart to inspect its GPU-computed range and the original sample ID of its peak.

Each channel retains three source chunks. A reusable command graph reads a shared GPU range and
reduces each channel into 1,024 bins. Rendering consumes those same output buffers. Changing the
viewport does not upload the source again or download chart coordinates. Only an explicit click
reads back one 40-byte summary. The chart uses fixed bin capacity, not a claim of one bin per device
pixel; each viewport change scans its visible samples. No GPU timing or speedup is implied.

The [GPU time-series API](/docs/api-reference/experimental/gpu-timeseries) documents the uniform
sample model, exact integer partitions, identity, missingness, ownership and cost.

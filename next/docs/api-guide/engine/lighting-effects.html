Combining Lighting, Shadows, and Ambient Occlusion

Start with a physically based material and explicit lighting. Add shadow maps for light visibility, contact shadows for short-range directional refinement, and ambient occlusion only when nearby surfaces need less indirect illumination. These effects solve different parts of the lighting calculation; enabling all of them is not a requirement.

The PBR Shadows example keeps these choices in one scene. Its bouncing sphere makes contact-shadow fade visible as the sphere lifts away from the plane. The same controls compare shadow maps, contact shadows, both, and ambient-occlusion quality without creating separate example applications.

Terms​

TermExpansionWhat it does here
PBRPhysically Based RenderingA material response driven by surface properties and illumination.
IBLImage-Based LightingIndirect illumination sampled from an environment map.
AOAmbient OcclusionApproximate nearby obstruction of indirect illumination.
SSAOScreen-Space Ambient OcclusionThe cheaper AO evaluator, sampling nearby depth values.
GTAOGround-Truth Ambient OcclusionThe higher-quality AO evaluator, integrating visible horizons. The name does not imply exact visibility of geometry outside the depth buffer.
PCFPercentage-Closer FilteringSmooths shadow-map visibility by combining depth comparisons.
PCSSPercentage-Closer Soft ShadowsEstimates blockers to vary shadow softness with separation.
HDRHigh Dynamic RangeLighting/color values with a wider intensity range than ordinary display color.
SDRStandard Dynamic RangeThe ordinary display range after exposure, tone mapping, and output transfer.

Which effect changes which light?​

MechanismInputsContribution affectedMain limitation
Environment lightingPrefiltered radiance, diffuse environment, materialAdds indirect diffuse/specular lightAn environment does not automatically capture local scene occlusion.
Shadow mapsLight-view geometry and depthEach supported light's direct contributionResolution, bias, light-view coverage and filtering.
Contact shadowsCamera depth/normals and one directional-light directionFirst directional light's direct contributionOnly surfaces visible in the current camera depth buffer can block rays.
AO visibilityCamera depth/normalsAmbient light, IBL and lightmaps in opt-in PBR receiversApproximation from a single visible depth layer; it does not add cast shadows for individual lights.
Material occlusion mapAuthored texture/UVsExisting material behaviorAuthored data cannot follow new object motion or scene changes.

Contact shadows combine with a directional shadow map using the minimum visibility, not by multiplying two estimates of the same direct-light occlusion. AO is applied separately to indirect contributions. Direct lights and emission retain their energy when screen-space AO changes. Authored material occlusion remains independent; avoid unintentionally stacking several strong approximations of the same crease.

AO choices: none, cheap, good​

ChoiceAPI modeUse whenCost and quality
None'none'The scene already reads clearly, authored lighting is sufficient, or the backend lacks AO support.Returns a constant unoccluded texture and records no AO draws.
Cheap'ssao'You want a modest contact/crease cue with a smaller evaluation budget.12 nearby depth samples followed by two depth-aware blur passes. Perspective camera only; radius is in pixels.
Good'gtao'Nearby surfaces need a more convincing horizon-based visibility estimate.Four slices, six samples per side, followed by the same two blur passes. Perspective camera only; radius is in view-space scene units.

These labels are quality choices, not a guarantee that one option always meets a frame-time budget. AmbientOcclusionPass defaults to GTAO at half resolution. Half resolution reduces intermediate pixel count to about one quarter; the returned mask is full input resolution. Small creases can soften at reduced resolution. Compare quality and frame time in your scene.

Both current AO evaluators require WebGPU. On WebGL2, select 'none' and keep rendering. The example exposes only that choice on WebGL2 and explains the limitation in its info panel. PBR mask receiving itself supports WebGL2 and WebGPU, so another producer may supply a mask.

Record one surface prepass, then share its outputs​

flowchart LR
  Geometry[Camera-visible PBR geometry] --> Surface[PBRSurfacePass]
  Surface --> Depth[Depth and view normals]
  Depth --> Contact[ContactShadowPass]
  Depth --> AO[AmbientOcclusionPass]
  Geometry --> Maps[Light-view shadow maps]
  Contact --> Receiver[PBR receivers]
  AO --> Receiver
  Maps --> Receiver
  Environment[Environment lighting] --> Receiver
  Receiver --> Output[Tone mapping and output transfer]

Record light-view shadow maps and the camera surface prepass before drawing receivers. Contact shadows and AO share camera depth and encoded view normals; neither owns or recaptures the scene. The receiver then accumulates lighting with the appropriate visibility factors. Tone mapping and output transfer happen once, after accumulation.

import {AmbientOcclusionPass} from '@luma.gl/engine/lighting';

const occlusion = new AmbientOcclusionPass(device, {
  mode: device.type === 'webgpu' ? 'gtao' : 'none',
  resolutionScale: 0.5
});

// Record surfaces.render(...) first. Use this same frame's camera projection.
const visibility = occlusion.render({
  depthTexture: surfaces.depthTexture,
  normalTexture: surfaces.normalTexture,
  projectionMatrix,
  near: 0.1,
  far: 40
});
// receiver was constructed with receiveAmbientOcclusion: true.
receiver.shaderInputs.setProps({
  pbrAmbientOcclusion: {
    ambientOcclusionTexture: visibility,
    viewport: [0, 0, width, height],
    strength: 1,
    enabled: true
  }
});
// Record receivers, then let the application submit.

Use physical pixels for both attachments and the receiver viewport. Depth, normals, projection and viewport must describe the same camera/frame. A nonzero viewport origin must be included in mask receiving props. SSAO near/far values must match its perspective projection. GTAO and contact shadows use the backend-native projection and its inverse.

Visibility versus color postprocessing​

Use AmbientOcclusionPass with PBR receiving when the material pipeline can apply visibility at light accumulation. It reuses the existing SSAO/GTAO evaluators and depth-aware blur; it does not introduce another AO algorithm. It has no temporal history or velocity input.

Existing createSSAOCompositeShaderPass and createGTAOCompositeShaderPass exports in @luma.gl/engine/effects remain available for postprocessing. Multiplying complete scene color also darkens direct light and emission. GTAO's composition: 'ambient-only' avoids that by requiring a separately supplied ambient-light color texture. This is a different contract from a visibility mask bound to a PBR receiver.

The existing GTAO composite includes temporal reprojection and needs a matching velocity texture. The reusable surface prepass currently supplies depth and normals, not motion vectors. Do not substitute zero velocities for moving objects or cameras to claim temporal stability. Integrating motion-vector production and shared history is a separate step.

Ownership, resizing, and tuning​

Passes borrow input attachments. Returned masks belong to their pass; rebind the returned texture each frame because intermediate/output storage may alternate. Input-size changes resize and replace owned allocations. Submit work before destroying/replacing referenced resources. Destroying a pass releases its targets and execution resources, not the caller's surface textures, materials, or receiver models.

Start with a short contact-ray distance and modest AO strength. Larger AO radius or contact distance cannot recover offscreen geometry and can exaggerate halos or thin-surface errors. Bias reduces self-occlusion at the cost of detached contacts. Thickness describes contact ray hits, not AO radius. Camera motion and disocclusion can reveal sampling noise; the new visibility passes intentionally contain no stale history.

Baked diffuse lighting​

Use LightmapBaker for static, UV-unwrapped surfaces. Record sampled lighting and shadow maps in texture space, accumulate irradiance, then bind the result to PBR materials. Reset after bake inputs change. Baked light can include direct illumination: disable corresponding live contributions to avoid counting them twice. Baking does not replace dynamic-object lighting or screen-space effects.

The Lightmap Baking example combines shadowed punctual lights and directional samples from sampleLightmapEnvironment. On WebGPU it also traces two diffuse bounces for indirect color bleed. The lightmaps guide walks through baking a glTF scene and reloading its HDR result.

Use readLightmapData and encodeLightmapData for explicit HDR export; reload with decodeLightmapData and createLightmapFromData. The returned result owns its GPU texture; CPU data and the matching geometry/UV layout remain application-owned. Exported irradiance is linear, without tone mapping. Changing the lights marks the example bake stale until reset.

Frame and resource lifecycle​

Keep one surface prepass per camera, shared by AO and contact shadows. Prepare model uniforms before opening its render pass. Record the visibility passes after surfaces, bind their returned textures to receivers, and then draw receivers. Shadow maps may be prepared earlier in the frame. Clustered light lists select light candidates independently of visibility; constructing a grid does not automatically make the standalone PBR shader consume it. The application still supplies its cluster-aware shading consumer.

Use one update/consume sequence per mutable pass or grid per submission. Uniform buffers and scratch targets are reused; recording several differently parameterized views before submitting can overwrite inputs needed by earlier work. Use separate instances for independent views.

ChangeRequired action
Camera resolution or device pixel ratioSubmit outstanding work, resize the surface pass in physical pixels, then render AO/contact again and rebind returned masks and receiver viewports.
Camera matrices without a resolution changeRefresh surface, visibility and receiver matrices together. Rebuild cluster lists using the matching projection and depth range.
Lighting, transforms or geometry used for a bakeReset the baker before its next update. Camera movement alone does not invalidate a world-space bake.
Atlas resolutionSubmit work referencing the old atlas, resize the baker, and reacquire its preview; resize also resets the sample count.
Replacing a material mapFollow the model's material-specialization/replacement contract; changing uniforms alone cannot add a shader feature.
Keeping a bake after producer disposalCall createResult(), submit the copy, then bind its independent texture. Destroy that result when its consumers no longer need it.

Same-size surface/baker resize calls preserve their allocations and bake sample count. Failed surface draw callbacks end their render pass, allowing later passes to be recorded. A failed surface/baker target allocation preserves the previous targets and dimensions and releases partial replacements. These guarantees do not make arbitrary GPU allocation failures recoverable at the application level; device loss still requires rebuilding device resources.

Destroy receiver and pass models when no recorded work references them. Materials borrow maps, visibility passes borrow surface attachments, and clustered grids borrow light-record buffers. Destroying those consumers does not transfer or release ownership of their inputs. A baker preview is borrowed and becomes invalid on replacement/disposal; a submitted result copy survives both.

Measure the complete workflow​

Tune one cost at a time in the existing example: start with AO/contact disabled, enable shadow maps, then contact shadows, then SSAO or GTAO. Keep camera, light count, shadow resolution, viewport and device pixel ratio fixed when comparing. Warm pipelines before collecting samples. Measure both CPU command recording and GPU execution; CPU performance.now() around recording alone does not measure GPU time. A readback fence measures completion plus synchronization and transfer overhead, not isolated shader execution. Use timestamp queries where supported to isolate GPU passes, and keep readback out of the production render loop.

The workflow regression provides a small reproducible diagnostic:

yarn test-headless --no-coverage --reporter=json --outputFile=lighting-workflow-results.json \
  modules/engine/test/lighting/lighting-workflow.spec.ts

It runs on baseline and max-feature WebGPU, plus WebGL for combined PBR receiving. The shared frame records surfaces, GTAO, contact shadows, clustered lists and a bake update. It verifies no new texture or persistent-buffer allocations after warm-up, and that ordered-upload staging buffers are destroyed after submission. Its timing annotations (also in JSON reporter test metadata) discard five warm-up frames and reports medians over twenty frames, including CPU recording/submission and completion with a one-pixel readback. These tiny fixtures test orchestration overhead, not scene throughput or a recommended frame budget; timings have no CI pass/fail threshold. Repeat at production dimensions and record browser, GPU, backend, feature level and power state before drawing performance conclusions.

Budget memory separately from timing. The following are logical storage sizes, excluding driver alignment, pipelines, uniform buffers and any caller-owned inputs:

AllocationStorage budget
Camera surfaces8 × width × height bytes for rgba8 normals and depth32 depth; about 15.8 MiB at 1920 × 1080.
Default baker targets56 × atlasWidth × atlasHeight bytes: three rgba16 targets and two rgba32 history targets; 56 MiB at 1024².
Independent default bake resultAnother 8 × atlasWidth × atlasHeight bytes; 8 MiB at 1024².
Cluster output and scratch4 × clusterCount × (1 + maxLightsPerCluster + ceil(maxLightCount / 32)) + 80 bytes; about 1.07 MiB at the default capacities, excluding compute-model resources.

AO/contact additionally allocate scaled evaluation/blur targets and full-size outputs. Half-scale intermediates have about one quarter as many texels, but full-size surfaces and returned masks remain. Increasing atlas width and height by two multiplies baker storage and raster work by four. Dilation adds one full-atlas pass per step; keep it explicit and measure it separately from sampling.
